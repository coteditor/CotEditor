Store settings in iCloud in CotEditor on Mac

You can store a copy of your CotEditor settings in iCloud. The copy is automatically updated when you change settings, and you can import it on another Mac whenever you want.

Tip: To use this feature, sign in to the same Apple Account and turn on iCloud Drive on each Mac. See Store files in iCloud Drive on Mac in the Mac User Guide.

Keep a copy of your settings in iCloud

  1. Go to the CotEditor app on the Mac whose settings you want to keep.

  2. Choose CotEditor > Settings, then click General.

  3. Turn on “Keep a copy in iCloud” in the Settings import & export section.

While this setting is on, CotEditor automatically updates the copy when you change any of the included settings. Each Mac and macOS user account has its own copy.

For a list of settings included in the copy, see Export and import settings.

To stop saving a copy, turn off “Keep a copy in iCloud” on the Mac it comes from. The existing copy remains in iCloud until you delete it.

Import settings from iCloud

  1. Go to the CotEditor app on the Mac where you want to import the settings.

  2. Choose CotEditor > Settings, then click General.

  3. Click the arrow next to Import in the Settings import & export section, then choose the settings copy you want to import.

  4. Select the items you want to import, then click Import.

The settings on this Mac are overwritten with the imported data.

You can import a copy even when “Keep a copy in iCloud” is off on this Mac.

Delete a settings copy from iCloud

To prevent the copy from being created again, first turn off “Keep a copy in iCloud” on the Mac it comes from.

  1. Go to the CotEditor app on your Mac.

  2. Choose CotEditor > Settings, then click General.

  3. Click the arrow next to Import in the Settings import & export section, then choose Manage Copies on iCloud.

  4. Select the copy you want to delete, then click Delete Copy.

  5. Click Delete in the confirmation dialog.

Deleting a copy doesn’t change the CotEditor settings on any Mac.
